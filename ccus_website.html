01 — Overview

What is CCUS?

Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) is a suite of technologies that intercept CO₂ at the point of emission or directly from the atmosphere, then either permanently store it underground or convert it into useful products. It is considered essential for decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors.

⚗️
Carbon Capture
Separation of CO₂ from flue gas or process streams using chemical absorption (amine scrubbing), physical adsorption, membranes, or cryogenic distillation. Typical removal efficiency: 85–95%.
🔄
CO₂ Compression & Transport
Captured CO₂ is dehydrated, compressed to supercritical phase (>73 bar, >31°C), and transported via pipeline, ship, or truck to storage or utilization sites.
⚡
Carbon Utilization
CO₂ as a feedstock: e-fuels, methanol, urea synthesis, enhanced oil recovery, concrete curing, food-grade CO₂, and emerging pathways like CO₂ to polymers or jet fuel (SAF).
🌍
Carbon Storage
Permanent geological sequestration in deep saline aquifers (>800m), depleted oil/gas reservoirs, or basaltic formations through mineral trapping reactions over geological time.
02 — Process

End-to-End Process Flow

🏭
Step 01
Emission Source
Power plants, cement, steel, fertilizer, refinery — point source or atmosphere
🧪
Step 02
Capture Unit
Pre/post-combustion or oxy-fuel; absorber/stripper columns; regeneration via steam
🔧
Step 03
Compression
Multi-stage compression to ~150 bar; dehydration to avoid hydrate formation in pipelines
♾️
Step 04
Store or Use
Geological injection >800 m depth, or conversion to products via chemical/biological pathways
03 — Capture Technologies

Capture Methods

Post-Combustion
Amine Scrubbing
Efficiency: 85–95% | Maturity: Commercial
Flue gas contacts MEA or advanced solvents in absorber column. CO₂ reacts chemically with amine. Rich solvent is thermally regenerated in stripper releasing pure CO₂.
Retrofittable to existing plants
High selectivity for CO₂
Energy penalty: 3–4 GJ/tCO₂
Pre-Combustion
Shift + Absorption
Efficiency: 85–90% | Maturity: Commercial
Fuel is reformed to syngas (H₂ + CO). Water-gas shift converts CO → CO₂. CO₂ removed via physical solvents (Selexol, Rectisol) at high pressure. H₂ fuel remains.
Lower energy penalty vs post-combustion
High CO₂ partial pressure aids separation
Enables blue hydrogen production
Oxy-Fuel
Oxy-Fuel Combustion
Efficiency: >95% | Maturity: Demo-scale
Combustion in pure O₂ (from ASU) instead of air. Flue gas is nearly pure CO₂ + H₂O. Water condensed out → CO₂ ready for compression. No separation column needed.
Near-pure CO₂ stream directly
Eliminates NOx formation
ASU is energy-intensive
Direct Air Capture
DAC — Solid/Liquid
CO₂ at 420 ppm | Maturity: Early Commercial
Contactors pull atmospheric CO₂ using solid sorbents (temperature swing) or liquid KOH (calcium caustic loop). Very energy intensive but enables negative emissions from anywhere.
Location-independent, no point source
Enables net-negative emissions
Current cost: $300–1000/tCO₂
Membrane Separation
Polymeric Membranes
Efficiency: 60–80% | Maturity: Emerging
Semi-permeable membranes with selective permeability for CO₂ vs N₂/CH₄. No regeneration needed — continuous operation. Best for high CO₂ concentration streams.
No phase change — compact footprint
Low maintenance, modular
Limited purity without multi-stage
Bioenergy + CCS
BECCS
Net-negative | Maturity: Emerging
Biomass absorbs CO₂ during growth. At conversion (combustion, fermentation), CO₂ is captured and stored. Biogenic carbon removal = net-negative. Fermentation CO₂ is near-pure.
Cheapest negative emission pathway
Ethanol fermentation CO₂ is near-pure
Land-use constraints apply

04 — Storage

Geological Storage Options

8,000+
Gt CO₂ — Global Capacity Estimate
Deep Saline Aquifers
Most abundant storage type. CO₂ injected >800m into porous rock capped by impermeable shale. Four trapping mechanisms: structural, residual, dissolution, and mineral trapping. Monitoring via 4D seismic and InSAR.
135
Gt CO₂ — Depleted Reservoir Capacity
Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR)
CO₂ injected into producing or depleted oil reservoirs swells oil, reduces viscosity, and improves recovery by 15–20%. CO₂ remains sequestered. Revenue offsets capture costs — most economically mature pathway.
2 yrs
Mineralization time in basalt
Basalt Mineral Trapping
CO₂ dissolved in water and injected into basaltic rock reacts to form stable carbonate minerals (calcite, magnesite) within 2 years. CarbFix project in Iceland demonstrated this. Permanent — no long-term monitoring needed.
>800
m — minimum injection depth
Depleted Gas Fields
Proven structural integrity from decades of natural gas containment. Excellent geological understanding from production data. Lower risk vs saline aquifers. CO₂ EGR (enhanced gas recovery) also being explored.
05 — Utilization

CO₂ as a Feedstock

CO₂ can be chemically, electrochemically, or biologically converted into fuels, chemicals, and materials — closing the carbon loop and generating economic value from emissions.

🔵
Methanol
CO₂ + 3H₂ → CH₃OH + H₂O. Used in fuel blending, chemical feedstock, marine fuel. George Olah Plant (Iceland) — commercial scale.
✈️
Synthetic Fuels (SAF)
Fischer-Tropsch or methanol-to-jet pathways using CO₂ + green H₂. Life-cycle emissions 70–80% lower than conventional jet fuel.
🌾
Urea & Fertilizer
CO₂ + NH₃ → Urea. Largest current CCU market (~2 Gt CO₂/yr). CO₂ sourced from steam methane reformer off-gas at fertilizer plants.
🏗️
Concrete Curing
CO₂ injected during curing mineralizes permanently. Improves compressive strength 10–15%. CarbonCure Technologies — commercial scale globally.
🧪
Polymers & Plastics
CO₂-based polycarbonates and polyols. Covestro Dream Production — CO₂ replaces 20% petroleum feedstock in polyurethane foams.
⚡
Power-to-X
Electrolysis of H₂O + CO₂ reduction (reverse WGS) → syngas for further synthesis. Enables seasonal energy storage via e-fuels.
🍺
Food & Beverage
Food-grade CO₂ for carbonation, modified atmosphere packaging, dry ice. High purity requirement. Small but established and stable market.
🌿
Enhanced Agriculture
CO₂ enrichment in greenhouses increases crop yields 20–40%. Direct use — no conversion. Localized demand near industrial point sources.
06 — Challenges

Key Engineering Challenges

01
Energy Penalty of Capture
Amine regeneration requires 3–4 GJ/tCO₂. For a 1 GW coal plant, this reduces net output by ~25–30%. Advanced solvents (piperazine, ionic liquids) target <2 GJ/tCO₂.
High
02
Cost — $/tCO₂ Captured
Post-combustion: $50–100/t. DAC: $300–1000/t. IEA target for DAC is <$100/t by 2030. Policy support (45Q tax credits) and scale are reducing costs steadily.
High
03
Pipeline Infrastructure
Supercritical CO₂ is corrosive to mild steel in presence of water. Requires dehydration and corrosion-resistant materials. US CO₂ pipeline network needs 10× expansion for net-zero scenario.
High
04
Long-term Storage Integrity
Caprock integrity, induced seismicity from injection pressure, and wellbore cement degradation are key risks. Monitoring requirements extend 50–100 years post-injection.
Medium
05
Solvent Degradation & Emissions
MEA degrades thermally and oxidatively forming nitrosamines (carcinogenic). Requires reclaimer units and solvent makeup. Advanced amines (piperazine, AMP blends) show improved stability.
Medium
06
Public Acceptance & Regulation
Social license for geological storage sites; liability frameworks for long-term CO₂ containment. Regulatory clarity needed for carbon markets and cross-border CO₂ transport.
Policy
07 — Industries

Hard-to-Abate Sectors

CCUS is considered essential — not optional — for these sectors where direct electrification cannot eliminate process emissions.

Cement
~65% process CO₂ (calcination)
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ cannot be avoided by switching fuels. CCUS is the only decarbonization route for process emissions. Heidelberg, Holcim piloting.
Steel (BF / DRI)
~7% of global CO₂
Blast furnace off-gas is CO₂-rich. Top gas recycling + CCS. Green steel (H₂-DRI) avoids CO₂ but CCUS bridges transition. SSAB, ArcelorMittal active projects.
Ammonia & Fertilizer
1.8% of global CO₂
SMR produces high-purity CO₂ stream — lowest-cost CCS opportunity. Blue ammonia already commercial. Saudi Arabia, Australia exporting blue NH₃ to Asia.
Natural Gas Power
~20% of global CO₂
Post-combustion amine scrubbing retrofits viable. Allam-Fetvedt cycle (NET Power) uses oxy-fuel with supercritical CO₂ turbine — near-zero emissions at competitive LCOE.
Refining & Petrochemicals
~4% of global CO₂
Multiple point sources on one site — hub-and-cluster model. Rotterdam CCUS cluster (Shell, Air Liquide) aggregates emissions for shared pipeline to North Sea storage.
Waste-to-Energy / BECCS
Net-negative pathway
Biogenic CO₂ from combustion or fermentation captured = negative emissions. Stockholm Exergi BECCS project targets 800,000 t/yr removal. Lowest-cost negative emission route.
